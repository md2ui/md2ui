# 2025 World Press Photo Winners - Top 10 Selection

**68th Annual World Press Photo Contest**  
**Announced:** April 17, 2025  
**Entries:** 59,320 photographs from 3,778 photographers across 141 countries  
**Total Winners:** 42 photographers from 30 countries

---

## 1. PHOTO OF THE YEAR - Winner 🏆

### "Mahmoud Ajjour, Aged Nine"

![Photo of the Year 2025](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/getmedia/b020e930-7993-4fdb-b93a-75d960dcf62e/WPP-2025Contest-POY-SamarAbuElouf.jpg?maxsidesize=1920&resizemode=force)

**Photographer:** Samar Abu Elouf (Palestine)  
**For:** The New York Times  
**Location:** Doha, Qatar  
**Date:** June 28, 2024

**Description:**

A profoundly moving portrait of nine-year-old Mahmoud Ajjour, who lost both arms while fleeing an Israeli attack in Gaza City in March 2024. The photograph shows the young boy in profile, soft light illuminating his face, revealing a melancholic expression that contrasts sharply with his young features. 

Mahmoud was severely injured when he turned back to urge his family onward during the attack; an explosion severed one arm and mutilated the other. He was evacuated to Doha, Qatar, for treatment. Photographer Samar Abu Elouf, also evacuated from Gaza in December 2023, now lives in the same apartment complex as Mahmoud and has been documenting the few badly wounded Gazans who made it out for medical care.

**Significance:** This quiet but powerful image encapsulates the devastating human cost of war on the youngest and most vulnerable. The jury praised it for providing "a layered entry point into a complex story" and demonstrating what great photojournalism can achieve.

**Award:** €10,000 cash prize + FUJIFILM GFX100 II camera with lenses

## 2. PHOTO OF THE YEAR - Finalist

### "Night Crossing"

![Night Crossing](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/night-crossing-2025.jpg)

**Photographer:** John Moore (United States)  
**For:** Getty Images  
**Location:** Campo, California, USA  
**Date:** March 7, 2024

**Description:**

Chinese migrants huddle together, warming themselves during a cold rain after crossing the US-Mexico border. The image captures a moment that is both otherworldly and intimate, depicting the complex realities of migration at the border—a topic often flattened and politicized in public discourse in the United States.

The photograph shows the human vulnerability behind migration statistics, revealing the physical hardship and uncertainty faced by those seeking a new life. The cold, wet conditions and the migrants' body language convey exhaustion, determination, and the harsh reality of border crossings.

**Significance:** This image challenges viewers to see migration as a deeply human story rather than an abstract political issue, highlighting the dangerous conditions migrants endure.

## 3. PHOTO OF THE YEAR - Finalist

### "Droughts in the Amazon"

![Droughts in the Amazon](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/amazon-drought-2025.jpg)

**Photographer:** Musuk Nolte (Peru/Mexico)  
**For:** Panos Pictures / Bertha Foundation  
**Location:** Manacapuru, Amazonas, Brazil  
**Date:** October 5, 2024

**Description:**

A young man brings food to his mother who lives in the village of Manacapuru. The village was once accessible by boat, but due to severe drought, he must now walk two kilometers along the dry riverbed of the Solimões River to reach her.

This powerful image makes the often abstract effects of climate change tangibly real, showing how environmental disasters directly impact daily life in vulnerable communities closely connected with the natural world. The vast expanse of cracked, dry earth where water once flowed illustrates the dramatic transformation of the Amazon ecosystem.

**Significance:** The photograph transforms climate change from distant statistics into immediate, visceral reality, demonstrating how it disrupts fundamental aspects of life for communities dependent on river systems.

## 4. Regional Winner - Africa

### "Women's Bodies as Battlefields"

![Women's Bodies as Battlefields](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/womens-bodies-battlefields.jpg)

**Photographer:** Cinzia Canneri (Italy)  
**For:** Association Camille Lepage  
**Location:** Addis Ababa, Ethiopia  
**Date:** October 31, 2017

**Description:**

Yohanna, 22, rests next to her mother after receiving treatment for complications arising from kidney removal. Shot by Eritrean police at the border, she woke up in a hospital where she learned that one of her kidneys had been removed without her consent.

This haunting image documents violence against women in conflict zones, revealing how women's bodies become literal battlegrounds in war and displacement. The intimate moment between mother and daughter speaks to resilience and family bonds amid unimaginable trauma.

**Significance:** The photograph exposes often-hidden atrocities committed against women during conflicts and displacement, giving voice to victims of gender-based violence in war zones.

## 5. Regional Winner - North and Central America

### "Attempted Assassination of Donald Trump"

![Trump Assassination Attempt](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/trump-assassination-2025.jpg)

**Photographer:** Jabin Botsford (United States)  
**For:** The Washington Post  
**Location:** Butler, Pennsylvania, USA  
**Date:** July 13, 2024

**Description:**

Members of the United States Secret Service help Republican presidential nominee Donald Trump off stage moments after a bullet from an attempted assassin grazed his ear during a campaign rally. The image captures the immediate chaos and protective response following the shooting.

This photograph documents a pivotal moment in American political history, showing the vulnerability of political figures and the swift action of security personnel. The image became one of the most widely circulated photos of 2024.

**Significance:** The photograph captures a defining moment in the 2024 US presidential campaign and raises questions about political violence in contemporary America.

## 6. Regional Winner - South America

### "Life and Death in a Country Without Constitutional Rights"

![El Salvador Prisons](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/el-salvador-prisons.jpg)

**Photographer:** Regional Winner (El Salvador coverage)  
**Location:** El Salvador  
**Date:** 2024

**Description:**

Documentation of life inside El Salvador's prison system, which has the highest incarceration rate in the world. Prisoners face extremely harsh conditions under President Nayib Bukele's controversial "war on gangs" policy.

The images reveal overcrowded facilities, strict security measures, and the human cost of mass incarceration policies. The photographs provide rare insight into a prison system that has been largely closed to outside observers.

**Significance:** The work documents a controversial approach to crime that has raised international human rights concerns while examining the broader implications of sacrificing civil liberties for security.

## 7. Regional Winner - Europe

### "Beyond the Trenches"

![Beyond the Trenches Ukraine](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/ukraine-beyond-trenches.jpg)

**Photographer:** Multiple contributors  
**Location:** Borshchivka, Ukraine  
**Date:** March 7, 2024

**Description:**

Anhelina, 6, who is traumatized and suffers panic attacks after having to flee her village near Kupiansk (a frontline city in Russia's invasion of Ukraine). She now lives with her grandmother in Borshchivka, 95 kilometers from Kupiansk. The photograph shows Anhelina in her new home.

This image captures the psychological toll of war on children, revealing trauma that will affect a generation of Ukrainian youth. The girl's expression and body language convey the deep emotional scars left by displacement and violence.

**Significance:** The photograph humanizes war statistics, showing that the conflict's impact extends far beyond frontlines into the daily lives and mental health of the most vulnerable.

## 8. Regional Winner - Asia-Pacific and Oceania

### "Four Storms, Ten Days"

![Philippines Typhoons](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/philippines-typhoons.jpg)

**Photographer:** Regional Winner  
**Location:** Ilagan City, Isabela, Philippines  
**Date:** November 12, 2024

**Description:**

A man wades through a street flooded by heavy rains from Typhoon Toraji in Ilagan City, northern Philippines. Four consecutive cyclones, three of which developed into typhoons, hit the Philippines in a matter of days in late October and early November 2024.

The image captures the relentless nature of climate-driven extreme weather events and their impact on communities. The lone figure wading through deep floodwaters illustrates both vulnerability and resilience in the face of natural disasters.

**Significance:** The photograph documents the increasing frequency and intensity of tropical storms linked to climate change, showing real-world impacts on vulnerable island nations.

## 9. Regional Winner - Africa

### "Wedding in Sudan"

![Sudanese Wedding](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/sudan-wedding.jpg)

**Photographer:** Mosab Abushama (Sudan)  
**Location:** Omdurman, Sudan  
**Date:** January 12, 2024

**Description:**

The groom poses for a portrait at his wedding. In Sudan, announcing a wedding with celebratory gunfire is a long-standing tradition. Remarkably, this photo was taken with a mobile phone camera, demonstrating that powerful photojournalism doesn't always require expensive equipment.

The image captures joy and tradition in a country experiencing ongoing conflict, offering a rare glimpse of normal life and cultural celebration amid turmoil. The photograph demonstrates how life, love, and tradition continue even in the most challenging circumstances.

**Significance:** The work shows resilience and the persistence of cultural traditions in conflict zones, while also proving that compelling photojournalism can be created with accessible technology.

## 10. Regional Winner - Multiple Categories

### "Protests in Kenya"

![Kenya Protests](https://www.worldpressphoto.org/kenya-protests.jpg)

**Photographer:** Luis Tato (Multiple images)  
**For:** Agence France-Presse  
**Location:** Nairobi, Kenya  
**Date:** June-July 2024

**Description:**

A series of powerful images documenting anti-government protests in Kenya, including:
- A protester raising a fist in defiance as Kenyan police officers use a water cannon with pink dye (used to mark individuals for identification) 
- Protesters chanting slogans and pushing makeshift barricades during clashes with police
- Kenyan police officers and security personnel protecting Parliament against protesters attempting to storm the building

The photographs capture a pivotal moment in Kenyan politics when citizens, particularly youth, took to the streets to protest government policies and corruption. Reports that police fired live ammunition at demonstrators led to international condemnation and calls for police reform.

**Significance:** These images document a critical moment in Kenya's democratic struggle, showing both the determination of protesters and the state's response to dissent.

## About the 2025 Contest

**Key Themes:**
- Conflict and war (Gaza, Ukraine, Sudan, Lebanon)
- Migration and displacement
- Climate crisis and environmental disasters
- Political upheaval and protests
- Gender rights and LGBTQI+ persecution
- Resilience, family, and community

**Contest Changes for 2025:**
- Increased from 33 to 42 total winners
- Three winners per region in Singles and Stories categories (previously one each)
- Continued emphasis on local photographers (30 of 42 winners photographed in their home countries)
- Strict prohibition on AI-generated or AI-enhanced images

**Regional Categories:**
1. Africa
2. Asia-Pacific and Oceania
3. Europe
4. North and Central America
5. South America
6. West, Central, and South Asia

## Jury Statement

**Lucy Conticello**, Global Jury Chair and Director of Photography for M (Le Monde's weekend magazine):

> "We made our choices with an eye on the final mix. As much as the World Press Photo Contest award is an immense recognition for photographers, often working under difficult circumstances, it is also a recap of the world's major events, however incomplete. As a jury we were looking for pictures that people can start conversations around."

**Joumana El Zein Khoury**, Executive Director of World Press Photo:

> "The world is not the same as it was in 1955 when World Press Photo was founded. We live in a time when it is easier than ever to look away, to scroll past, to disengage. But these images do not let us do that. They cut through the noise, forcing us to acknowledge what is unfolding, even when it is uncomfortable, even when it makes us question the world we live in—and our own role within it."
